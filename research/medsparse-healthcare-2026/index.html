MedSparse Healthcare: A Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Medical Language Model Trained from Scratch on Consumer Hardware

Sudhanshu Shukla · Independent researcher, Bengaluru, India

Download PDF · 8 pages DOI · 10.5281/zenodo.24461541 ↗ LaTeX source Code ↗ Models ↗ MIRIAD dataset ↗ Try it in the browser

Abstract

We describe MedSparse Healthcare, a 38.8M-parameter decoder-only language model whose feed-forward layers are sparse mixtures of experts (four experts per layer, top-2 routing), trained from random initialization on healthcare text with a 30,001-token byte-level BPE tokenizer learned only from biomedical documents. Stage 1 pretrains on openly licensed PubMed full text from the Common Pile; Stage 2 applies full-parameter, completion-only supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on medical question–answer pairs. Evaluated with a float32 inference engine that runs in the browser, Stage 2 lowers held-out medical answer perplexity from 692.5 to 252.9 (ratio 0.365, 95% CI 0.343–0.389) while PubMed perplexity rises by 17% (ratio 1.166, 95% CI 1.087–1.244), and expert routing stays balanced (normalized routing entropy ≥ 0.966 in every layer). Multiple-choice accuracy on 150 MedMCQA validation questions (32.7% and 28.7%) is not distinguishable from the 25% chance level and stays below the 38% majority-answer baseline, which we report as a limitation of models at this scale. Stage 3 continues full-parameter SFT from the Stage 2 checkpoint on the 5.8 million MIRIAD question–answer pairs, with a validation split that keeps whole source papers out of training, for 1,507,000 optimizer steps on a single consumer-grade GPU. On the same held-out items it lowers medical answer perplexity further to 59.6 (ratio to Stage 2 0.236, 95% CI 0.194–0.287), brings PubMed perplexity back to 585.6 (ratio 0.878, 95% CI 0.710–1.080) and raises answer top-1 next-token accuracy to 33.8%; on 15 MIRIAD questions from held-out papers its reference-answer perplexity is 9.5, against 383 for Stage 2. MedMCQA accuracy (31.3%) remains at chance, and an informal reading of 15 generated answers found 3 correct or mostly correct and 7 wrong. We release the weights under the Apache License 2.0, together with the training code, the evaluation harness, and practical lessons for training on consumer hardware. The model is a research artifact and is not intended for clinical use.

Status

Version 1.0. Complete: all three releases, including Stage 3 on the MIRIAD 5.8M dataset (model card), evaluated with the same harness. Known limits: MIRIAD coverage was not exact after a resume bug (Section 7), and standard medical QA suites such as MedQA and PubMedQA are not yet run.

Responsible use

MedSparse Healthcare is a research model. It is not a medical device and must not be used for diagnosis, treatment, triage, medication dosing or patient communication. It is an independent implementation inspired by the Mixtral architecture; it does not use or distribute Mistral AI or Mixtral weights and is not affiliated with Mistral AI.

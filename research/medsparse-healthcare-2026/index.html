MedSparse Healthcare: A Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Medical Language Model Trained from Scratch on Consumer Hardware

Sudhanshu Shukla · Independent researcher, Bengaluru, India

Download PDF · 7 pages LaTeX source Code ↗ Models ↗ Try it in the browser

Abstract

We describe MedSparse Healthcare, a 38.8M-parameter decoder-only language model whose feed-forward layers are sparse mixtures of experts (four experts per layer, top-2 routing), trained from random initialization on healthcare text with a 30,001-token byte-level BPE tokenizer learned only from biomedical documents. Stage 1 pretrains on openly licensed PubMed full text from the Common Pile; Stage 2 applies full-parameter, completion-only supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on medical question–answer pairs. Evaluated with a float32 inference engine that runs in the browser, Stage 2 lowers held-out medical answer perplexity from 692.5 to 252.9 (ratio 0.365, 95% CI 0.343–0.389) while PubMed perplexity rises by 17% (ratio 1.166, 95% CI 1.087–1.244), and expert routing stays balanced (normalized routing entropy ≥ 0.966 in every layer). Multiple-choice accuracy on 150 MedMCQA validation questions (32.7% and 28.7%) is not distinguishable from the 25% chance level and stays below the 38% majority-answer baseline, which we report as a limitation of models at this scale. Stage 3 continues full-parameter SFT over all 5.8 million MIRIAD question–answer pairs, with a validation split that keeps whole source papers out of training, on a single consumer laptop GPU; it is in progress, and its results will be reported in version 1.0 of this report. We release the weights under the Apache License 2.0, together with the training code, the evaluation harness, and practical lessons for training on consumer hardware. The model is a research artifact and is not intended for clinical use.

Responsible use

MedSparse Healthcare is a research model. It is not a medical device and must not be used for diagnosis, treatment, triage, medication dosing or patient communication. It is an independent implementation inspired by the Mixtral architecture; it does not use or distribute Mistral AI or Mixtral weights and is not affiliated with Mistral AI.

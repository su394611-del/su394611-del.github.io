OncoTrace: Source-Traced Evidence Summaries for Cancer Vaccines and Emerging Therapies

A research prototype from AI Lab for summarizing published oncology evidence, with each claim linked to the paper, trial record, or passage it came from.

New clinical trial readouts make oncology evidence harder to keep up with. OncoTrace combines retrieval over trial registries and research papers with MedSparse Healthcare, AI Lab’s sparse mixture-of-experts language model. Its first goal is to measure how often a cited passage actually supports the claim the model makes.

Target launch around 11 December 2026. GitHub and demo links are pending.

Read the design notes Evaluation Roadmap

Why now

In 2026, the first positive Phase 3 results for an individualized mRNA neoantigen therapy were presented at ESMO. Earlier-stage studies and registry updates across tumour types make it increasingly important to distinguish what was measured, where, and when. [1]

  • Melanoma, Phase 3. In INTerpath-001, intismeran autogene (mRNA-4157/V940) plus pembrolizumab met endpoints for recurrence-free and distant metastasis-free survival in patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV melanoma. Results were presented at ESMO 2026 in Madrid. [1, 2]
  • Pancreatic cancer, early evidence. In a small Phase 1 study of autogene cevumeran, 8 of 16 vaccinated participants developed vaccine-induced T-cell responses. Longer follow-up was discussed at AACR 2026, and a randomized Phase 2 study is registered as NCT05968326. [3–5]
  • Other tumour types. The broader clinical programme includes studies in lung, kidney and bladder cancers. [2, 6]

These candidates are investigational. Abstracts, registry records, sponsor releases and journal articles can differ in how and when they report the same study.

The problem

A fluent summary can blend sources or present an early result as current. OncoTrace tracks three errors a reviewer can check directly.

  1. Unsupported claims. No retrieved source supports the stated effect or endpoint.
  2. Misattribution. The claim points to the wrong trial, phase, or tumour type.
  3. Stale evidence. An older result is presented as the latest or as confirmed.

Research question. How often can a small medical language model produce claims a reviewer can verify against the cited source in one click?

How it works

Sources
PubMed abstracts, ClinicalTrials.gov records with NCT identifiers, open-access reviews, and conference abstracts.
Retrieval
Combine keyword and dense search while keeping trial phase, tumour type, status, and date available as filters.
Generation
MedSparse Healthcare drafts claims with inline citations to retrieved passages.
Verification
A second pass checks each claim against the exact passage cited and flags or removes unsupported claims.
Output
A short summary and a side-by-side evidence table with claims, source passages, dates, and trial phases where applicable.

Example research questions

  • What Phase 3 evidence exists for personalized mRNA vaccines in melanoma?
  • What phase and recruitment status do public pancreatic cancer vaccine trial records report?
  • How do recent reviews describe the role of AI in neoantigen prediction?

Evaluation

The planned test set pairs hand-checked questions with reference passages and groups them by tumour type and trial phase. The release milestone is a measured citation-faithfulness score with examples of failures.

Evaluation set size and scores are pending.

MetricWhat it measures
Citation precisionShare of claims fully supported by the cited passage.
Citation recallShare of key reference facts included in the summary.
Unsupported claim rateShare of claims without support in the retrieved sources.
Misattribution rateShare of correct facts attributed to the wrong trial or phase.
Recency accuracyShare of summaries that correctly report the latest phase and readout.

Baselines. Retrieval-augmented generation without verification, MedSparse Healthcare without retrieval, and a similarly sized open-weight model using the same retrieval.

Failure analysis. The report will pair failed claims with their cited passages and explain each error.

Related work

  • Neoantigen prediction. pVACtools and CNNeoPP are examples of prediction tools in the surrounding research literature. [7, 8]
  • AI in vaccine research. Recent reviews describe potential applications of AI and the challenge of validating predictions. [9]
  • Attributed generation. ALCE and MIRAGE provide reference points for citation quality and medical retrieval evaluation. [10, 11]

OncoTrace’s scope is reviewing published evidence and checking how accurately it is cited.

Roadmap and limitations

  1. December 2026 preview. Summaries from a curated corpus, evaluation results, and examples of failures.
  2. Next. Trial-registry updates that flag changes in study status or posted results.
  3. Later. Cross-trial comparisons and an open benchmark.

Known limitations

  • Coverage is limited to English-language, open-access sources.
  • Abstracts and sponsor announcements can overstate or omit details. A supported citation shows what a source says.
  • The model can misread tables, subgroup results, and survival statistics.
  • The planned evaluation uses a small, hand-built set and will need independent review and expansion.
